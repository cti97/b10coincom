What it is, and what it deliberately is not

The design

Built for small computers

Validating and storing a chain costs disk and bandwidth — a Pi does that fine. Producing blocks is where the cost lives, so b10coin moves the difficulty out of consensus and into issuance and distribution: a capped emission schedule and a keyless claim faucet.

  • Tendermint-style BFT: propose, prevote, precommit, commit
  • A dumb, token-gated relay so validators behind NAT can reach each other
  • Staking, epochs and committee rotation — one stake, one seat, capped power
  • Bounded history with verifiable checkpoints
The trade-off, stated

Permissionless, fairly distributed, no PoW — pick two

The design document makes the bargain explicit rather than hiding it. b10coin begins federated: a genesis committee of held public keys, with staking-based admission from there. Where proof of work survives — in the faucet — it is a rate limiter on claims, never a consensus mechanism.

  • No smart contracts, no privacy layer, no scaling side-chain
  • No governance token, no sale, no mainnet
  • Testnet coins are valueless by construction

How a block happens

01 · PROPOSE

The round proposer builds

One seat, chosen per height and round, assembles a block from its mempool and signs it.

02 · PREVOTE

Every validator judges

The block is re-validated against the chain — parent, height, proposer seat, signatures, state root — before a prevote is cast.

03 · PRECOMMIT

A quorum locks

Once prevotes reach quorum the validators lock and precommit. A lock is a promise; unlocking needs proof of a later round.

04 · COMMIT

State advances

Precommits reaching quorum commit the block, advance the state root, and start the next height at round zero.

Claims pay 1 b10 from protocol emission and cost an Argon2id proof — memory-hard, so a Pi completes one in seconds and a GPU farm gains little. Transactions pay a minimum fee of one spark; a bond of 1,000 b10 buys a seat, and no seat may hold more than a quarter of committee power once the committee has four or more members.

Where the project is

Running now

Live three-seat testnet

Three validators on three different home networks, one relay on a public VPS, all behind NAT. The chain has been committing continuously since the v0.1.8 transport fix — — blocks at the last check.

Fixture committee: every key derives from a public seed, so this proves the plumbing, not the security model. A separate committee-file mode uses held keys.

This is a testnet. Coins on it are valueless test currency. There is no sale, no token offering and no mainnet. The fixture committee's private keys are derived from a public seed published in the repository — anyone can sign as any seat. Do not treat this chain as securing anything of value.
